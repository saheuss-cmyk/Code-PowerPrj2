Overview

Our model is designed to identify live images of kitchen cleaning supplies. We focused less on capturing a wide variety of kitchen supplies and more on ensuring that each of our classifications was trained on diverse and inclusive data. We chose three unique yet variable classifications: dish soap, spray bottle, and sponge.

In order to limit potential training bias, we followed guidelines laid out by AI activist Joy Buolamwini. This led us to train our model on a variety of dish soaps, sponges, and spray bottles in different colors, shapes, lighting conditions, and background colors. We acknowledge that our model may still be less accurate for users of differing localities or cultures, as we were limited to training on objects that were easily accessible to UW-Madison students.

Our model performed well in tests, correctly identifying a variety of dish soaps, spray bottles, and sponges. Diversifying our training data significantly improved initial problems with identification accuracy. Regardless, the model continues to display some “flickering” between correct and incorrect identifications, and performs best when using solid-color, high-contrast backgrounds paired with a high-quality video feed. Future directions include adding more training data with lower quality images and low-contrast backgrounds, more data for our “none of the above” classification, and soliciting cross-cultural images of cleaning supplies to reduce bias.

Training Data

Our full training dataset can be found here. Each folder represents a different classification, and each zip file contains roughly a hundred images of a single object. Zip files are named according to shape, color, lighting, or background. The “none of the above” zip file contains images of a variety of different non-listed and/or non-cleaning items.


Our model is open-source, and we welcome improvements and expansions. Our full Github repository can be found here.
