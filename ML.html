Project Overview


During this project, our group decided to use kitchen cleaning supplies as items to train our machine. We cared less about what we were using and more about how we were training it. Items that we tested include multiple-colored sponges, dish soaps, and cleaning sprays. Although we had no intention of correlating kitchen cleaning products with specific content we discussed in this course, like Joy Buolamwini’s Unmasking AI, our intentions of diversifying our data were influenced by several aspects that Buolamwini touches on. What I mean by this is that we could have chosen anything, but rather we focused on making sure each item was represented by several different versions of it. There is a major importance of having diversity in datasets to accurately detect all subjects correctly. For example, in the Coded Bias film with Buolamwini, she talked about how the primary reason for face detection not working accurately on darker-toned faces was that the datasets were not studying enough diverse images. As we were collecting image data, we prioritized not only grabbing different kitchen cleaning supplies, but also different versions of the same cleaning supply. For example, we had four different spray bottles, three of which were different colors, and two of which were the same color. We did this so that our machine could recognize different types of spray bottles regardless of their color.
